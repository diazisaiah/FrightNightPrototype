## Model and rig architecture

Build a playground-owned production-style rig rather than expanding the existing emote-only pivot sculpture.

Use a shared named humanoid skeleton contract so animations can be reused and future network messages can refer to stable states. At minimum provide:

- `root`
- `pelvis`
- two or three spine/chest bones
- `neck` and `head`
- left/right clavicle
- left/right upper arm, forearm, wrist, and hand
- left/right thigh, shin, ankle, and foot
- optional attachment bones for headband tails, sashes, robe panels, cape pieces, long hair, and beard sections

The models must use detailed faceted polygon construction:

- Treat the voxel images as rendered character-design sheets, not instructions to recreate cube topology.
- Build high-resolution faceted polygon anatomy, faces, hair, cloth volume, beveled garment panels, wraps, gloves, and footwear. Curved forms should contain enough facets to reproduce the source silhouette cleanly at close inspection distance.
- Preserve flat polygon shading, controlled facet variation, rough materials, contact shadowing, and restrained edge highlights; do not turn the cast into smooth plastic mannequins.
- Weight every rigidly authored body or costume section to the appropriate semantic joint. Keep deformation predictable and avoid melting or shearing distinctive shapes around joints.
- Hide joints through sensible overlap: gloves, cuffs, wraps, shoulders, knees, belts, robe panels, and other existing design elements.
- Avoid visible holes during ordinary punch, guard, locomotion, hit, and knockdown poses.
- Do not create one node per facet or accessory detail. Bake each occupied joint's authored forms into a small number of combined mesh surfaces.
- Prefer one opaque vertex-colored surface per occupied joint plus limited emissive/effect surfaces and only the necessary animated attachments.

All six models must share bone names and state-machine semantics, but their rest poses, bone lengths, shoulder widths, head proportions, clothing meshes, and attachment locations may and should differ.

Design the factory cleanly enough that future fighters could eventually be added through a fighter definition and bespoke mesh without rewriting the controller, but do not implement, prototype, design, or generate art for any new fighter in this task.

## Animation architecture

Use `AnimationPlayer` plus `AnimationTree`, or an equivalently clear Godot-native state machine. Do not make gameplay depend on animation completion callbacks alone.

Separate simulation timing from visual playback:

- Gameplay/simulation owns movement, hit validity, damage, knockback, invulnerability, and ultimate results.
- Animation consumes an action name, hand, direction, severity, start time, and normalized phase.
- Clips must expose or document windup, active/impact, and recovery timing.
- Locomotion should not use uncontrolled root motion. The character controller remains authoritative over world position.
- Special moves that translate the player must follow simulation-controlled movement and let the animation visually match it.

Provide a reusable command-facing API similar in spirit to:

```gdscript
play_action({
    "action": "hook",
    "hand": "right",
    "started_at": server_or_local_time,
    "speed": 1.0,
    "direction": Vector3.FORWARD,
    "severity": 0.0,
})
```

The exact API may differ, but it must be compact enough that future multiplayer code can replicate state and events rather than bone transforms.
